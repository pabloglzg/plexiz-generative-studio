# > why 'write like x' never works

describing a style doesn't work. 'casual but smart' tells claude nothing. 'average sentence length 11 words, comma density 3.8 per 100, short sentence ratio 45%, zero hedging language' tells claude everything.

style is data. it lives in sentence rhythm, punctuation patterns, word complexity, paragraph length, and the verbal tics a writer repeats without knowing it.

the problem is most people can't see those patterns in writing they admire. they feel the voice but can't measure it. claude can measure it in seconds.

# > the forensic analyst prompt

paste this into claude with your sample text:

> "analyze the writing sample below as a forensic style analyst. measure and report: average sentence length, short sentence ratio (under 8 words), comma density per 100 words, vocabulary complexity, tone markers, hedging language frequency, and any verbal tics or signature phrases. label this the style dna. then write 300 words on [new topic] matching those exact metrics. finish with a side-by-side comparison showing accuracy percentage for each metric."

## here's what it does, step by step:

you paste 500 or more words of the target style. claude analyzes it for sentence rhythm, punctuation density, word complexity, paragraph length, tone markers, and signature phrases. the verbal tics that make a voice unmistakable.

then it shows you the style dna: a clean breakdown of every metric it measured.

you give it a new topic. it writes 300 words in the cloned style, matching the exact metrics it analyzed. then it shows you a side-by-side comparison with accuracy percentage.

(this sounds obvious but it isn't. the comparison is what makes this cracked. you can see exactly where the clone drifts from the original.)

## this is the part nobody talks about.

## > what the style dna actually looks like

after analyzing a sample, claude gives you something like this: average sentence length 12 words. short sentence ratio 40%. comma density 4.2 per 100 words. vocabulary: simple, concrete, no technical jargon. tone: direct, slightly informal, zero hedging. verbal tics: repeating the subject at the start of consecutive sentences for rhythm.

most writers have no idea their own style looks like this. they just write. claude finds the pattern underneath.

# > 3 steps to steal any voice

1. paste 500 or more words of the target style. the more the better. longer samples give claude more data to work with, so the clone is sharper.

2. 2. let claude analyze the patterns. don't skip the style dna step. this is where you find out what actually makes the voice distinctive, not what you think makes it distinctive.

3. 3. test it on a new topic. give claude something the original writer never wrote about. that's the real test. if the clone holds up on unfamiliar territory, you've got the voice.

# what to do with it

ghost-writing that sounds like the client, not like you trying to sound like the client. guest posts that match the publication's voice exactly. emails that still sound like you when you're running on empty.

it's also a learning tool. clone five different writers and you'll understand more about style than most people learn in years of reading. you start to see the mechanics underneath the voice.
